## Job Market Paper

[**The Value of a Hospital Obstetric Unit: Access, Costs, and Health**](/files/yu_thomas_jmp.pdf){:target="_blank"}

**Abstract:** I study OB provision, hospitals’ decisions to operate obstetric (OB) units. OB provision rates are lower among rural hospitals than urban hospitals and declining overall. How does OB provision affect access to care, the costs of delivering care, and maternal and infant health in equilibrium? Using births and medical claims data, I estimate a model of the hospital OB care market in Arkansas. I find that OB provision benefits mothers substantially by giving them access to more options and shortening travel distances, while effects on health and variable costs are mixed and small. The average OB unit generates a positive externality worth $102 per Arkansas birth, mostly driven by access to care. I evaluate an Arkansas policy that increases Medicaid prices to encourage OB provision. As OB care becomes less profitable, the policy prevents OB unit closures, returning $1.1 to $1.7 in benefits per dollar of spending.
